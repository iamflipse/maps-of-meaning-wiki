# 49. Elijah's Still, Small Voice, and Moses Striking the Rock

**Part 10: The Passion and the Foundation of Order** · transcript words 89,196–91,252 (2,057 words)

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

At the transfiguration Moses (the law) and Elijah (the prophets) appear. The Israelites demanded a king though they did not need one, and got Ahab, weak like Adam, seduced by Jezebel, a worshiper of the nature god Baal. Elijah defeats the prophets of Baal when fire consumes his water-soaked sacrifice, then, hiding from Jezebel, sees fire and earthquake and realizes God is not in nature's awe-inspiring forces but in the still, small voice: he identifies conscience with the divine, as Socrates did with his daemon. Awe, felt as hair standing on end, is an ancient emotion pointing beyond us. Cardinal Newman argued for God from conscience; someone without conscience is a psychopath. We are a collection of sub-personalities whose mature unity is not power or whim. At the edge of the promised land Moses strikes the rock instead of speaking, using force, and may not enter. Hierarchies rest on competence and reputation, not dominance.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- If you are responsible enough, you don't need a king.
- The divine is not in nature's spectacle but in the still, small voice of conscience.
- Awe points to what is beyond us and invites imitation.
- Maturity unifies our sub-personalities, not under power or whim.
- Healthy hierarchies are built on generous competence, not force.

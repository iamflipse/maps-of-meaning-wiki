## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Everything left unattended in your past remains as chaos, and part of the mind tracks it, raising stress and negative emotion. In religious practice and psychotherapy you revisit painful memories, which are territories you did not master, and ask where your aim was misaligned; solved, a memory loses its charge, because memory exists to extract wisdom for the future. Behavior therapy breaks feared things into small exposures: an OCD client began by touching a new garbage can with a pool cue. Imagined exposure works too, more so when vivid, as in Edna Foa's work with trauma survivors. Exposure to the future works as well: write a vision that makes you move. Map the past, confront the present and envision the future, and you build a walled garden, paradise.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Unresolved past chaos keeps raising your stress.
- Revisit painful memories to find where your aim was off; then they lose their charge.
- Break avoided fears into tiny steps you can actually take.
- Vivid imagined exposure also heals.
- Map the past, face the present, envision the future: then you're not a slave.

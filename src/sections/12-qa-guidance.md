# 12. Questions: Where to Find Guidance, Calling and Conscience

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

In a question period, Peterson is asked how to find a goal when you lack experience. His rule of thumb: if you don't know what you're doing, do what other people do. He mentions his Future Authoring program and lists what people do: work or a vocation, an intimate relationship, friendships, education and self-improvement, avoiding major temptations like alcoholism and sexual misbehavior, serving their community and caring for body and mind. He reports research showing creative achievement follows a Pareto distribution, with 70% scoring zero. Asked what changed since he wrote Maps of Meaning, he says he understood calling then, through 'The Divinity of Interest' and later the burning bush, but missed conscience, Elijah's 'still, small voice'. Guidance needs both, like mercy and justice.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- If you don't know what to do, start with what other people do.
- Work, relationships, friends, learning, avoiding temptation, service and health are a generic start.
- Strong creativity is rare; even exceptional people need a stable base.
- Calling is what grips your interest; pursue one thing deeply.
- Conscience, the still small voice, is the other half of guidance.

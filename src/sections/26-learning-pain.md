## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Peterson reviews the frame, its disruption and the meta-story, and relates it to Thomas Kuhn's scientific revolutions and Piaget's stage transitions, preferring Piaget's view that the new stage keeps the old one's features and adds the new. Frames nest upward (a wife, children, fatherhood, citizenship, the mythic narrative) and downward into micro-skills. Every act of learning kills a preconception, which is why people resist it: pain is proportional to the disruption. Stay current and fix problems while the dragon is small, biting off as much as you can chew. That sense of engagement signals an optimal rate of transformation. Because philosophy, neuroscience and narrative converge, he argues the pattern is true, as converging senses tell us what is real. Next come the characters of narrative.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Learning follows the pattern of revolution: collapse and reconstitution.
- A good new stage keeps everything the old one had and adds more.
- Learning hurts in proportion to how much it disrupts; that is why people resist.
- Pay attention and fix things while the dragon is still small.
- Engagement signals you have found the optimal rate of change.

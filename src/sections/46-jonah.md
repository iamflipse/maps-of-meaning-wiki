## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Conscience is the voice of your problems: what bothers you about yourself, others or the world. Asking for no problems may mean forgoing all opportunity, since treasure lies where dragons are; what interests and what bothers you mark the path. Jonah, an ordinary good man, is told to warn Nineveh, the enemy city, and understandably flees toward Tarshish and falls asleep, the unconsciousness of running from conscience. A storm threatens the ship, the ship of state; pagan sailors find him out, and he asks to be thrown overboard. A whale, the dragon of the depths, takes him for three days in hell, worse than death. He repents, is delivered, and goes to Nineveh, where he is now convincing because he has been to the bottom of things; the city repents and is saved, though Jonah is irritated it worked. A medieval image shows him emerging with a halo.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Conscience is the voice of your problems.
- What interests and what bothers you mark the path through chaos.
- Running from conscience brings unconsciousness, then a storm.
- Denying conscience can sink the whole ship of state.
- Having been to the bottom of things, you speak with authority.

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Peterson tells the story of Jacob, a usurping mama's boy who took Esau's birthright for lentil stew and his blessing in disguise, splitting an already fractured family. Fleeing Esau, Jacob resolves to change and builds an altar. A sacrifice is what you give up to reach an aim; all work sacrifices other possibilities. Cain's sacrifices are rejected because he does not bring his best, and instead of learning he blames God and invites in sin, 'crouching at the door'. Peterson contrasts this with the Marxist account of resentment and cites Solzhenitsyn on going along with lies. Jacob instead reappraises and dreams of the ladder. God is defined as the essence of all good things and is ultimately incomprehensible, as Moses glimpses only his back. Inspiration is a message from a higher state.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- A sacrifice is what you give up to accomplish an aim; all work is sacrifice.
- The first question of conscience: are you bringing your best to the table?
- Resentment comes from refusing to learn and inviting in the spirit of blame.
- When you fail, reappraise and aim up, as Jacob did.
- God can be defined as the essence of what all good things share.

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Peterson begins naming the characters of the narrative world. The outermost container is the dragon of chaos, which guards a treasure: possibility kills or gives. Heavenly treasure, stored where rust cannot reach, is a stellar reputation, like the best hunter who shares the best cut and stores provision in others' trust. Women look at wealth as a marker of the ability to generate it; why settle for the wealth when you could have its source? Creation myths show gods fighting chaos (Leviathan, Marduk and Tiamat) and tyranny (Behemoth, giants). Next comes the feminine, which gives birth, nurtures and can destroy, and which selects. Human female choosiness and sexual selection, Peterson argues, mean consciousness shapes evolution, as seen in butterflies and pufferfish sculptures.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Chaos is pictured as a dragon guarding treasure.
- Heavenly treasure is a trustworthy reputation, the basis of social harmony.
- Seek the source of wealth rather than the wealth itself.
- Heroes fight two foes: chaos and tyrannical order.
- The feminine gives birth, nurtures, destroys and selects.

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Adam's role in Genesis is to name and subdue, to specify and order. Eve is 'ezer kenegdo', which Peterson renders as an optimal partner in combat or play. He suggests the feminine speaks for what the current order leaves out, like a baby's needs, which gives it a protest function that can become usurping. Adam's weakness in eating the fruit matches Eve's sin, and his blaming of Eve and of God is pathetic. Both Lucifer, the intellect, and the maternal go most wrong when they overreach. You should not take pride in what you were given, such as a high IQ, and compassion is not the highest virtue.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Adam's task is to name and put things in order.
- Eve as 'ezer kenegdo': a partner who challenges you to grow.
- The feminine voices what the order excludes, and that can turn into usurping.
- Adam's weakness and blame are as bad as Eve's sin.
- Do not take pride in gifts you were given; serve with them.

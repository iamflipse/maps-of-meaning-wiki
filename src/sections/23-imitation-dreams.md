# 23. Status, Dreams and the Spirit of the Father: From Action to Philosophy

**Part 4: Sacrifice and the Ladder of Aims** · transcript words 46,192–48,015 (1,824 words)

← [22. Three Memory Systems: Skill Before Words, and Listening](22-memory-systems.md) · [24. The Meta-Story: Mastering Transformation Itself](24-meta-story.md) →

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

People are constantly shaping you, and status matters enormously to social animals: green monkeys look longest at high-status members, and wolves act out dominance and restraint without representing them. Humans represent the patterns others teach us, so our dreams and fantasies draw on a storehouse of who we should be; the essence of that is a religious image, deviation from it is conscience and attraction to it is calling. Elders are those people turn to in crisis. Knowledge moves from action to dream to image to explicit words. A child playing house imitates the abstracted spirit of the father, not his father's posture. Crowds in a stadium act out worship of hitting the aim. The West is fractured because it criticizes religious systems that abstract its procedural wisdom. The sequence runs from imitation to play, ritual, drama, myth, religion, literature and philosophy.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Social status and reputation are deeply important to primates, including us.
- Our dreams draw on everything others have shown us about who we should be.
- Knowledge moves from action to image to words.
- Children imitate an abstracted pattern, the spirit of the father, in play.
- Admiration is the instinct to imitate mastery of the known and unknown.

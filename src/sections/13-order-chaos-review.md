# 13. Characters, Depth and the Religious Level

**Part 3: Depth, Malevolence and the Sacred** · transcript words 24,260–26,399 (2,140 words)

← [12. Questions: Where to Find Guidance, Calling and Conscience](12-qa-guidance.md) · [14. Attention, the Two Hemispheres and Getting to the Bottom of Things](14-attention.md) →

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Reviewing, Peterson restates that experience is made of order, where what you want happens, and chaos, where things go wrong. A failure may only mean a change of strategy, or that the game itself has collapsed. He moves toward how this world is represented in characters and narrative, and offers a technical definition of the religious: our ideas have tiers of dependency, and the deepest are sacred. Betrayal adds malevolence to chaos, which can traumatize the naive. Badly hurt clients fell into religious language because only good and evil fit. He reviews the frame (Heidegger's Dasein), the motivational reference point, and the serpent in the garden: no garden without snakes. Master the game and the space between games; if you are bored, your attitude is wrong.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- A setback can be a strategic error or the collapse of a whole game.
- Deeper beliefs have more depending on them; the deepest are religious.
- Chaos includes malevolence, which can traumatize the naive.
- Every enclosure excludes imperfectly, so the unknown returns.
- Be at home in both explored and unexplored territory.

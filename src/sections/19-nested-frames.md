## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Frames of reference are nested. To teach a small child to clean up or set the table, you break the task into steps they can already do, starting from 'see that teddy bear?'. Behavior therapy does the same: shrink the task until the person does it, and improvement then accelerates rather than growing linearly. Being a good person decomposes into good roles, such as good parent, down to cutting vegetables, where heaven meets earth in action. Small acts like setting a knife on the table connect upward through shared meals, conversation and service to the highest good. The right attitude matters too, like children thanking their mother. Applied to a job interview, he urges aiming up, offering real value and making yourself radically useful once hired: arriving early and staying late. The firstborn is consecrated because the first decision sets the frame.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Teach and learn by decomposing a big aim into doable steps.
- Shrink the task until you do it; progress then accelerates.
- Being good decomposes into concrete actions at the bottom of the ladder.
- Small acts link upward to the highest aims.
- Bring an aim-up attitude to new opportunities and make yourself radically useful.

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Peterson recaps the narrative world: the dragon of chaos as the ground of all order, enclosed by the unknown (feminine symbolism), the known, and the knower, the hero, whose path leads to 'life eternal': the pattern that always works. Hercules, the Great Father and the mother of the hero are reviewed. In Genesis 1 the spirit over the waters, the Word and Adam's naming are one pattern, a description of consciousness, which meets the unknown and gives it form; how that goes depends on intent, as at a party. Gods fight Leviathan and Behemoth; St. George, England's patron, slays the dragon and founds a new order. The crucifix is death itself, a deeper abyss than the dragon, and resurrection means nothing is beyond transformation by love and attention. If nothing is more real than pain, what overcomes pain is more real. Ursula in The Little Mermaid fits the same pattern.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- The hero's path is the pattern that always works.
- Consciousness encounters the unknown and casts it into form.
- How possibility unfolds depends on your intent.
- The dragon slayer always founds a new order.
- Whatever overcomes pain is more real than pain.

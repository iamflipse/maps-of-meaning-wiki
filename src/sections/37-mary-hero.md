**Part 8: The Hero** · transcript words 70,142–71,551 (1,410 words)

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Out of chaos emerges the feminine, and its positive face is the mother of the redeeming hero: every infant is a possible redemption and every mother is Mary, who nurtures and then encourages, as at the wedding at Cana. Hercules, afloat in a protective vessel, wears the skin of the lion he overcame and carries a bow and a club covered in eyes: armed and awake. The walled garden's dragon returns; the hero leaves safety, faces it and finds gold and maidens, like Pinocchio freeing his father from the whale. The bigger the dragon you voluntarily confront, the more potential is released, as in the gym. Look for meaning in what bothers you and what interests you: 'Why not you?' Every small learning is a mini-revolution, so stay alert, with an eagle eye.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Every mother is the mother of a possible hero: nurture, then encourage.
- The hero is armed and awake.
- Facing the dragon frees the father: you discover who you are.
- The bigger the dragon you face voluntarily, the more potential is released.
- What bothers you is your problem; what interests you is your calling.

# 35. Venus, the Frightened Cat, Kali and Diana

**Part 7: The Characters of Myth** · transcript words 66,032–68,948 (2,917 words)

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Behind the evil queen is chaos itself, which is why she becomes a dragon; abstracting the obstacle lets you try to solve all problems at once, a religious question. A medieval Venus in a mandorla radiates the numinous, Rudolf Otto's term for what you cannot habituate to, like fire; for men she is also judgment and the terror of rejection. A hippie comic's cat, a real cat on walks and rats facing a cat in their enclosure show how animals flee the unknown, then cautiously re-explore it. Children populate the dark with monsters: teach them to handle monsters. The Hindu goddess Kali, spider in a web of fire, pictures horror; horror films let young people face fear and disgust, and eye-rolling contempt predicts divorce. Offering your best in the face of horror reveals the positive face, the many-breasted Diana.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Behind every personified evil stands chaos itself.
- The numinous is what you can never become used to.
- Animals and people retreat from the unknown, then re-explore it bit by bit.
- Teach children how to handle monsters, not that monsters don't exist.
- Face horror with your best and the welcoming face can emerge.

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Honoring your parents means looking at what they did with discrimination, separating wheat from chaff. Horus, the god of attention, can see evil. He returns to fight Seth and loses an eye, because no one fights the corrupting spirit without damage. He wins the eye back but, instead of keeping it, descends to the underworld and gives it to the blind Osiris: the hero gives tradition vision, uniting the living and the dead. The Pharaoh embodies the union of the restored state and the exploratory hero, and that idea of sovereignty spread down Egyptian society toward the notion of the equal divinity of all. A culture's vitality depends on the narrative it is embedded in.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Honor your parents by discerning what to keep and what to discard.
- Fighting corruption will cost you; you will not come out unscathed.
- The hero's role is to give tradition vision, not just to rule.
- Sovereignty is the union of renewed tradition and courageous exploration.
- A culture lives as long as it keeps faith with its founding story.

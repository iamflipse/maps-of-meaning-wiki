# 51. It's Up to You: Facing Evil, and Why Exposure Makes You Braver

**Part 10: The Passion and the Foundation of Order** · transcript words 92,439–93,971 (1,533 words)

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Peterson wanted to understand how ordinary people became the guards at Auschwitz and in the gulags, and to point to a response, because evil must be acted on. That burden, Atlas's world and Christ's cross, is up to you: when good people stay silent hell dominates, and maybe one person can stop it. You got where you are by not facing things, and you cannot transform anything without facing it. He describes an Orthodox triptych of Christ before the cross, inviting, and Simba invited down into chaos. Everything bottoms out in mystery, which he defines as God. He says he tried to break each claim and kept only what he could not doubt. Behavior therapy found relaxation unnecessary: voluntary exposure works and accidental exposure makes people worse; people become braver rather than less afraid, and that generalizes, so agoraphobic clients began speaking up in their marriages.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Evil must be understood and acted on; that responsibility is yours.
- When good people remain silent, hell dominates.
- You cannot transform anything without facing it.
- Only voluntary exposure heals; accidental exposure makes things worse.
- Exposure makes people braver, and bravery generalizes.

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Rejecting any uniting narrative leaves power and pleasure as organizers. Peterson reads the Book of Revelation as the pattern of how things end, not the end of time: the scarlet, many-headed beast is a state whose center has collapsed, and on its back rides the whore of Babylon, the commodified sexuality of a degenerate city. In the end the beast kills the prostitute. He ties this to falling marriage and birth rates and the prevalence of online pornography, and recommends the film Cabaret as a portrait of the dance between tyranny and hedonism, with the master of ceremonies as an agent of downward transformation.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Without a uniting principle, power and pleasure take over.
- Revelation describes how things end, as Genesis describes how they begin.
- A many-headed state has no direction.
- Commodified sexuality accompanies a disintegrated order.
- Tyranny and hedonism feed each other.

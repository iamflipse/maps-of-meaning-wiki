# 5. The Hierarchy of Games and Agents of Magical Transformation

**Part 1: The Frame of Perception** · transcript words 8,994–11,244 (2,251 words)

← [4. Pathways, Tools and Obstacles: Conscience and Calling](04-pathways-tools.md) · [6. Setting the Bar and the Unexpected Event](06-setting-the-bar.md) →

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Games are defined by rules, which are enabling principles rather than restrictions. Games can be ranked: some degenerate, some improve as they are played, and the best improve while bringing in more people. The talented but selfish athlete in the sports movie learns there are higher games than winning. The game is the metagame, which is why it matters how you play. An agent of magical transformation resets your aim, like Gandalf calling Bilbo out of the Shire. Peterson discusses Nietzsche on cowardice posing as morality, integrating the shadow, the dragon and treasure story, the basilisk and the phoenix, death and resurrection, Jacob's ladder, Dante's Inferno, Lucifer, Christ's temptations and Douglas Murray's conclusion about evil. Downward agents tempt toward worse games.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Rules are enabling principles that let a game exist.
- The best games improve as they are played and include more people.
- The metagame matters more than any single game: how you play.
- An agent of magical transformation resets your aim, for better or worse.
- Things fall apart and reconstitute, ideally at a higher level each time.

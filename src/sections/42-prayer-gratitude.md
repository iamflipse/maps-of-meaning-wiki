## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Asked why one person sees an obstacle where another sees a tool, Peterson says part is temperament (extraversion, low neuroticism) but gratitude can be practiced until it becomes automatic, which is what prayer is for. He reads Christ's three temptations as rejecting God as a wish-granting machine, a rescuer from self-made disaster, or a source of power. Pray for sharper aim, humility and gratitude: the spirit of your aim is the divinity that answers your prayer, and 'how am I an idiot?' will always be answered. Asked about his most formidable dragon, he describes his daughter's severe childhood arthritis and the all-beef diet the family credits with her recovery, and the lesson from public attacks: stay on track, don't apologize or cede ground, aim properly and wait.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Temperament sets a default, but gratitude can be trained.
- Prayer is not wish-granting; Christ rejects that in the desert.
- Pray for sharper aim, humility and gratitude.
- The spirit of your aim is what answers you.
- Under attack, stay on track, aim properly and be patient.

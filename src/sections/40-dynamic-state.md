## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

What if the new state put the process that transforms states in the highest place? Nietzsche saw that meeting other cultures raises doubt about one's own principles and then about all of them, leading to nihilism. The remedy is to put faith not in a static system but in voluntary death and resurrection, a meta-state, like dancing to changing music. Christ heals on the Sabbath and, in a story outside the gospels, tells a man rescuing a sheep that he is blessed if he knows what he is doing; the highest service is to the spirit that gives rise to the law, which is why he calls the Pharisees whitewashed tombs. Being social is sacrificial: a three-year-old learns to take turns, and a generous child gains friends and more games. Tyrants reach brutal ends; the hero's courage dwarfs the tyrant's.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Faith belongs in the process of transformation, not a fixed system.
- Serve the spirit that gave rise to the law, not just the letter.
- Voluntary sacrifice toward the highest unites the psyche and the state.
- Maturity means subordinating the present to the long term.
- Generosity in small things builds friends and a reputation.

# 4. Pathways, Tools and Obstacles: Conscience and Calling

**Part 1: The Frame of Perception** · transcript words 6,643–8,993 (2,351 words)

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Returning to the basic frame, Peterson draws on J.J. Gibson's ecological approach to perception and the neuropsychology of Jeffrey Gray and Jaak Panksepp. We perceive in relation to our aim: people become obstacles when they block us, and emotions calibrate whether we are on track. We first see pathways, which raises the question of what to aim at so the pathway beckons. Using Pinocchio and Jiminy Cricket, he describes a dialogue with conscience. Conscience speaks through negative emotion and calling through enthusiasm, like the pillars of cloud and fire. The best path is tilted upward to force growth, and motivation is strongest when you run from something terrible toward something good. We see tools rather than objects, and in the social world friends and foes.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Things in your way produce negative emotion; things that move you forward produce positive emotion.
- The first things we perceive are pathways.
- Conscience works through negative emotion; calling through positive emotion.
- A good path is challenging enough to compel development.
- We see objects as tools, obstacles, friends and foes, defined by function.

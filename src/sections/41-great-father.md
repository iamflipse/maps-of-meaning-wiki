## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Order has two faces: security and tyranny. Walls keep you in but keep monsters out. The wise king offers security with permeable walls, so children grow brave enough to leave or build their own. Tyranny's psychological cost is stultification: under totalitarianism, suffering is declared illegal, and Peterson defines hell as being punished for admitting your misery. The wise king holds a globe with a cross and a scepter like Moses' living staff. The evil king devours his son out of jealousy. The hero has two sets of parents, nature and culture; Jung urged forgiving your father for not being God, and honoring your parents means preserving what was good with gratitude. The 14th-century vierge ouvrante shows God and the crucifixion inside Mary: Western towns were built around the image of voluntary acceptance of the worst.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Order provides both security and tyranny.
- Give children security with permeable walls so they can become brave.
- Tyranny forbids admitting suffering; that is a kind of hell.
- Everyone has two sets of parents: nature and culture.
- Forgive your father for not being God, and carry forward what was good.

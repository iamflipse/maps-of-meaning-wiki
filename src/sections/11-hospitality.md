## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Peterson summarizes the mythological landscape: chaos and order, hell to heaven, pathways, tools and characters. Planting a flag claims order from chaos. From visiting clients' homes, he describes houses full of untouched boxes, each holding a hundred avoided decisions, where everyone is stressed and ill, and the opposite, homes so orderly no one can step out of line. The goal is balance, signaled by play and meaning and by hospitality, among the highest Old Testament virtues. He tells how Abraham welcomed three strangers who were angels or God, bargained for Sodom down to ten good people, and how hospitality kept peace between settlements. The moral: you are meant to be one of the ten.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Myth maps the world as order surrounded by chaos.
- Clutter is a pile of avoided decisions, and it causes stress.
- Too much order is as stressful as too much chaos.
- Balance shows up as play, meaning and hospitality.
- A few good people can save a corrupt city; aim to be one of them.

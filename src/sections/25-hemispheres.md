# 25. Novelty and Routine: The Hemispheres, Neglect and the Homunculus

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Drawing on the Russian tradition (Sokolov's orienting response, Luria, and Elkhonon Goldberg), Peterson explains that the right hemisphere is specialized for novelty and unexplored territory and the left for routine, like a bird with one eye on food and one on predators. As a skill is learned, brain activation shifts left and shrinks into an automatic machine, so beware of what you practice. The left hemisphere is efficient but narrow; with right parietal stroke, patients deny their paralysis because the old map persists. He mentions Iain McGilchrist's The Master and His Emissary. He then shows Penfield's homunculus: we are mostly hands, eyes, lips and tongue, unlike dolphins, which is how we engage the world as active explorers.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- The right hemisphere handles novelty; the left handles routine.
- Practiced actions become automatic machines in the brain.
- Efficiency in the wrong direction is the worst outcome.
- Damage to the novelty system leaves people blind to catastrophe.
- Hands, eyes and speech make us explorers of order and chaos.

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Freed slaves are lost, quarrelsome and nostalgic for tyranny; it takes forty years, three generations, to cross a desert that is a few weeks' walk. Moses judges every dispute from dawn to dark until his father-in-law Jethro tells him he will wear himself out and steal the people's destiny. Jethro proposes groups of ten with elected representatives up to the thousands: subsidiarity, devolved responsibility, the alternative to tyrant and slave. After years of judging, Moses receives the Ten Commandments, the procedures of justice cast into words. Worship means aiming at and sacrificing toward; work sacrifices the present for the future. Removing a tyrant leaves only slaves. The Passion is the story of facing the worst death and malevolence for the highest good, the same voluntary confrontation that drives learning and therapy.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- People freed from tyranny are lost before they can govern themselves.
- Subsidiarity: push responsibility down to the lowest workable level.
- Judging thousands of disputes reveals the principles of justice.
- Work is sacrifice of the present for the future.
- You cannot master what you shy away from.

# About and Sources

## What is here

- **54 section pages** following Jordan B. Peterson's Maps of Meaning lecture series, "Two Ways of Looking at the World", in lecture order, grouped into 11 parts. Each has a summary and key ideas in plain words (paraphrase), two to four short quotes (each under about 30 words), questions to explore and related links.
- **40 concept pages** that follow one idea across the whole series, with two to four quotes and the list of sections where it comes up.
- **Study aids:** a cheat sheet, a themes index (common search words mapped to concepts and sections), all section quotes in one place, and a coverage map of transcript word ranges.

The full lecture transcript is **not** published here.

## The lectures

- **Speaker:** Jordan B. Peterson. The series is his Maps of Meaning material (the ideas of his 1999 book *Maps of Meaning: The Architecture of Belief* and the university course of the same name), delivered as a lecture series that opens with the claim that there are two fundamental ways of looking at the world: as a place of things, or as a forum for action.
- **What it covers:** perception and aim, order and chaos, the nested hierarchy of games and goals, sacrifice, the meta-story of descent and renewal, the archetypes (great mother, great father, hero, adversary), and readings of Genesis, Cain and Abel, Jacob, Exodus, Job, Jonah, Elijah, the brazen serpent and the Passion alongside Mesopotamian and Egyptian myth. Several question-and-answer sessions are included.

## How this wiki was made

- **Transcript:** a private transcript of about 98,803 words (whitespace normalized), kept in a local study wiki (`~/wikis/two-ways-of-looking/`). That wiki splits the transcript into 54 contiguous sections in 11 parts, each with a summary, key ideas, exact quotes with word positions, and questions; its coverage audit confirms every word belongs to exactly one section.
- **From there to here:** section and concept pages are built from those notes. Summaries and key ideas are paraphrase, not Peterson's words. Quotes were re-checked against the whitespace-normalized transcript as exact text; only quotes of 30 words or fewer were kept, and two to four were chosen per page, spread across the section. Word positions ("about word N") count words from the start of the transcript and are approximate.
- **Titles:** the transcript has no headings, so every section, part and concept title is added by this wiki.

## Caveats

The pages are a reader's summary of a lecture series, not Peterson's own words except inside quotation marks. They reflect his interpretation of myth and scripture, which is psychological rather than doctrinal. For his full argument, go to the lectures and the book themselves.

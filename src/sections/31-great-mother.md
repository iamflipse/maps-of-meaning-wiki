## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Bowerbirds build and dance for choosy females. Peterson argues nature is pictured as mother because it selects, and rejection tells a man he will not replicate. The thorns around Sleeping Beauty's castle test whether the prince can get through, and the right male response is 'it's you'. The dragon of chaos is the hypothetical place of all obstacles and treasures; the unknown as encountered takes on a feminine character because male and female are among the most fundamental perceptual categories. Society, the patriarchy in his terms, shields individuals from nature, as when God clothes Adam and Eve in skins. This raises the question of what principle of social organization keeps nature from overwhelming us.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Nature is symbolized as mother because it selects.
- Facing rejection, the useful response is to look to your own insufficiency.
- The dragon of chaos is the abstract place of all obstacles and treasures.
- Male and female are basic categories the imagination uses to represent the abstract.
- Social organization is the human way of adapting to nature.

# 36. Job and the Passion: Faith in the Face of Catastrophe

**Part 7: The Characters of Myth** · transcript words 68,949–70,141 (1,193 words)

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

God boasts of Job; Satan bets he is only fortunate. Job loses his children, fortune and health, his friends blame him and his wife says 'curse God and die'. Job decides, as an act of faith, that he will not lose faith in his own essential goodness or in the benevolence of being: aim up, no matter what. The Passion magnifies Job into a limit story, the worst fate on the least deserving person, which Jung called a tragicomedy. Catastrophe will come; the question is how to bear it. Peterson contrasts his wife's family, who drew together during their mother's long illness, with families who fall into resentment, bitterness and revenge. Nature, too, is both benevolent and cruel at once.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Misfortune can strike even when you do everything right.
- Job chooses faith in his own goodness and in the goodness of being.
- The Passion is the limit case of undeserved suffering.
- How you bear catastrophe shapes everyone around you.
- Resentment leads to bitterness, revenge and a hell worse than the tragedy.

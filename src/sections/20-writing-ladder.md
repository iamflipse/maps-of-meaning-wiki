## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Writing shows how levels nest: letters, words, phrases, sentences and the book, inside the role of scientist, professor, citizen and good person, which touches the mythic. Getting one word right means taking all those levels into account. William Blake's image shows the ladder rising to the sun, a symbol of value like a gold coin. Sisyphus can be reimagined as a series of ever higher hills: the goal is not satiation but expanded adventure. Identity is nested from father and husband out to the exploratory hero, which is why we identify with mythic heroes; Simba in The Lion King descends, sees his father, and remembers who he is. A society or psyche needs unity of purpose at every level. Facing a challenge voluntarily turns on different genes than shying away, which Peterson links to epigenetics.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Every right word draws on all the levels of purpose above it.
- Your goal is not satiation but expanded adventure.
- Your personal identity overlaps at its edge with mythic identity.
- Religious education connects you with the eternal in you.
- Unity of purpose must reach every level of a person or society.

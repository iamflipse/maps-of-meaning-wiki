# 15. Time for a New Game: The Beginning, the Fall and the Serpent

**Part 3: Depth, Malevolence and the Sacred** · transcript words 28,333–30,805 (2,473 words)

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

When all pathways are blocked, an animal dies but a human can find a new aim: a new game, story, character and spirit. Abraham and Jacob are renamed after their transformations. Following Mircea Eliade, every collapse is a return to the beginning, pictured as the primordial waters over which the spirit of God hovers. John identifies that spirit with voluntary self-sacrifice, like a parent's love, so the rule is aim up. The fall is biting off more than you can chew. The serpent and the dragon are images of the predator, built from ancient threats to primates, and the worst predator is the serpent in one's own heart, as the catastrophes of the 20th century show. The road to perdition is many small avoidances. The word that orders chaos must be true, and faith is trusting that telling the truth leads to the best outcome.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- A human whose pathways are blocked can find a new aim and become a new person.
- Every collapse returns you to the beginning, the realm of possibility.
- The spirit that brings good order out of chaos is love and voluntary self-sacrifice: aim up.
- The dragon is the image of the predator; the worst predator is in the heart.
- Perdition comes through many small acts of avoidance; truth is the foundation.

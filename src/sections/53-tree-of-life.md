## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Closing images merge the tree of life and the tree of knowledge: Christ crucified on the tree of life, with the serpent below handing Eve the fruit. Christ is the brazen serpent transformed and a second, bitter-looking fruit that is the antidote to pride and the fall, which the Mass dramatizes. Voluntary self-sacrifice in service of the highest is the orienting principle of the natural, social and psychological order. The flaming sword at Eden's gate burns away everything unworthy; 'be ye therefore perfect'. Understanding is meta-food, and the most effective food is trust: high-trust societies are abundant. We acted these patterns out, then pictured them, and only later understand them. You can master what you confront; admiration for those who faced the worst is the instinct to imitate, where biology and the highest calling meet. Raise your children properly and transform the world. He says he has attacked these ideas from many angles for 40 years.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- The cross is the tree of life; its fruit is the antidote to the fall.
- Voluntary self-sacrifice toward the highest is the orienting principle.
- Offering your best means burning away what is unworthy.
- Trust is the most effective food: high-trust societies are abundant.
- You can master what you confront.

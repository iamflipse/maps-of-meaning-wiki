## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Understanding the deep structure of stories adds a layer to enjoying them without spoiling them. Peterson credits Jung's student Erich Neumann (The Great Mother, The Origins and History of Consciousness), and links them with Campbell's work, Jung's Symbols of Transformation and Maps of Meaning. Camille Paglia told him that if academics had turned to Neumann, Eliade and Jung rather than Foucault and Derrida, recent history would differ. He rejects the postmodern claim that there is no uniting meta-narrative: the uniting narrative is the story of how frames transform. After Nietzsche's 'death of God', the question is what principle should unite a society; power can unite for a time, as with dominant chimpanzees, but is not optimal.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Knowing a story's deep structure adds understanding without spoiling it.
- Neumann, Jung, Eliade and Campbell all mapped the hero's path.
- Peterson rejects the postmodern claim of no uniting meta-narrative.
- The uniting narrative describes how frames of reference transform.
- Power can unite a group for a while, but is far from optimal.

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Peterson describes three memory systems using the patient H.M., who lost both hippocampi yet learned a star-maze skill without remembering it. Procedural memory holds skills like riding a bike, ballistic movements and walking. Declarative memory has episodic (image) and semantic (word) parts. Piaget found children could play marbles together before they could state its rules: we act before we can represent. This raises the question of how dreams can teach what we seem not to know. People shape each other constantly with attention and disappointment, following an ideal pattern. One thing they look for is whether you can listen: listening reveals what people want, and the wise salesman listens for real problems to build trust rather than to make a single sale.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Procedural memory (skill) is separate from declarative memory (images and words).
- Skill is embodied; you cannot fully tell someone how to ride a bike.
- Children act out rules before they can state them.
- Others shape us constantly through attention, following an ideal pattern.
- If you listen to people, they will tell you what they want.

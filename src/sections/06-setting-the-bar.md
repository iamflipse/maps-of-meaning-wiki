# 6. Setting the Bar and the Unexpected Event

**Part 1: The Frame of Perception** · transcript words 11,245–13,324 (2,080 words)

← [5. The Hierarchy of Games and Agents of Magical Transformation](05-games.md) · [7. Anomaly: Dragon and Treasure](07-anomaly.md) →

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Looking at the frame emotionally, the present is neutral or negative compared with the destination, and that polarity drives you forward. If you are not doing something, lower the bar to half or a quarter until you move. Peterson describes behavioral therapy as negotiating small tasks, Pinocchio fleeing then facing Monstro, a client who had not cleaned her room in decades, and an agoraphobic client who called an elevator a tomb. He then turns to prediction: a good plan gets you to the goal predictably, but an unexpected event, like a broken-down car for a struggling 35-year-old, can open a rabbit hole of layered problems beneath the surface.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- The gap between what is and what should be supplies your drive.
- If you are stuck, lower the bar until you start moving.
- A small step that turns descent into ascent can be a revolutionary move.
- Small trivial problems often sit on top of deep layered ones.
- Unexpected events can force a change of strategy, aim, or deeper aim.

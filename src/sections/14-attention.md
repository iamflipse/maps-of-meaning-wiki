# 14. Attention, the Two Hemispheres and Getting to the Bottom of Things

**Part 3: Depth, Malevolence and the Sacred** · transcript words 26,400–28,332 (1,933 words)

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

If something bores you, change what you look at or how you look. In therapy, Peterson learned that if a conversation did not chase away competing thoughts, he was not listening hard enough. The Egyptians worshiped attention as Horus, the open eye; upward-oriented attention is close to love, and attention turns a house into a home. Because the world is made of order and chaos, the brain has two hemispheres specialized for each, and even artificial networks need a stable module and an exception module (the penguin problem). The Quidditch seeker plays a metagame for the snitch, an alchemical symbol, like Moses attending to the burning bush. Take one thing seriously and get to the bottom of it; any upward-aiming discipline can lead to wisdom, through apprenticeship then mastery.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- If you are bored, change what you look at or how you look at it.
- Attention oriented upward is close to love.
- A home is a house where everything has been attended to.
- The brain's two hemispheres are adapted to order and to chaos.
- Get great at one thing; deep focus generalizes into wisdom.

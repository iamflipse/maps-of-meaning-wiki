## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

In the Mesopotamian creation story, Tiamat, the feminine dragon of salt water, and her consort Apsu give rise to the gods, primordial forces like war (which Jane Goodall found even in chimpanzees). The noisy gods kill Apsu, a recurring 'death of God': they destroy the foundation they stand on. Tiamat returns like a flood, as in the children's book There's No Such Thing as Dragons, where an ignored dragon grows until it carries off the house and shrinks once acknowledged. Osiris's willful blindness speeds decay; universities that drive out their spirit leave only a carcass. Marduk, with eyes all round his head and magic words, is chosen to determine destiny; he nets Tiamat, cuts her up and makes the world, and makes man from the blood of Kingu, which Peterson links to the human capacity for malevolence, born of knowing we can be hurt.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Killing the founding father destroys the ground you stand on.
- Ignored problems grow like dragons; acknowledged ones shrink.
- Willful blindness speeds the decay of any order.
- Order comes from chaos through attention and the right word.
- Knowing we can be hurt makes malevolence possible.

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

The basic cast is chaos (the dragon and its treasure), the feminine (creation and destruction), the patriarchal (order and tyranny), and the hero with his adversary, as in Cain and Abel, Christ and Satan, superhero and supervillain; plots are their combinations. The tail-eating dragon pictures self-propagating feedback loops such as drinking to cure a hangover, depression and spreading avoidance. The Great Father gathers images of order (city, culture, day sky, Osiris); the Great Mother gathers the unknown (nature, matter, dark water, Isis, the grave). Symbols are clusters of associated words, which large language models make measurable. Bukowski's poem 'Nirvana', about a hospitable diner, is contrasted with Peterson's account of diners run by people who hate people. Narrative describes our frames, and 'it's just entertainment' is, he says, a foolish theory.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Chaos, the feminine, the masculine, and hero and adversary form the basic cast.
- Each character has a positive and a negative form.
- The tail-eating dragon pictures self-reinforcing downward spirals.
- A symbol is a cluster of associated meanings.
- How people run even a diner can make it a small heaven or hell.

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Peterson asks what frames the frame. Behaviorists tried to explain behavior as chains of reflexes (B.F. Skinner's training of pigeons and rats) but failed, shown by a rat that ran a maze on a cart. He defines a motivation as an instinctual goal, like hunger, that competes to take over your frame, and lists basic states mediated by ancient brain systems such as the hypothalamus and the dopamine system. Motivations act like sub-personalities that possess you, as anger does, and archaic gods personify them. He discusses Nietzsche, Freud and Jung, Eve's temptation and pride, sin as missing the mark, and atonement as fixing a misaligned aim. A good aim must work for you and others now and in the long run, which is what play signals.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Behavior cannot be explained as deterministic chains of reflexes.
- A motivation is better seen as an instinctual goal than a drive.
- Motivational states act like sub-personalities that take over your perception.
- Sin means missing the mark; atonement replaces a misaligned aim with a higher one.
- A good aim works for you and for others, now and in the long run.

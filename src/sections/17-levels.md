## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Peterson's computer died in 1980s Montreal; checking the fuse, the house and the street, he learned a solar event had caused a vast blackout, a lesson in nested dependencies. Myth pictures catastrophe as the evil queen of nature or the blindness of the king, and he argues the New Orleans flood was also a failure of integrity. When a frame collapses, you search up and down the hierarchy for the right level to solve the problem, as with a broken car or a marital argument, where you should not start with divorce; beware the fundamental attribution error. We exist at every level from quantum to cosmic, and health, including mental health, is harmony across all of them, like music. The Norse world tree Yggdrasil pictures this structure, with serpents at the roots and order at the center.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Everything predictable depends on deeper constants we ignore until they fail.
- Disasters can reflect the integrity of the people in charge.
- Solve problems at the simplest level that works; don't start with the foundation.
- We exist at many nested levels and bear some responsibility for each.
- Health is harmony across all those levels, not just something inside you.

# 52. Mary, the Scouts of Canaan, and Faith in the Future

**Part 10: The Passion and the Foundation of Order** · transcript words 93,972–96,084 (2,113 words)

## What Peterson says (paraphrase)

Mary suffers with Christ; the worst torture is to threaten a child. Peterson calls 'who would bring a child into a world like this?' cowardice and faithlessness rather than benevolence. Of the scouts Moses sends into Canaan, ten report giants and doom; Caleb and Joshua say that if they aim up and keep the covenant, no desert can't bloom, and they lead the people in: maintain faith in the future, choose life. He argues anti-natalism leads toward demoralizing women and young men and working against what thrives. Michelangelo's Pietà is the female equivalent of the crucifixion; the cure is not removing serpents but fostering the courage to handle them. Knowing there are things worse than death lets you see malevolence. The resurrection is a mystery, but the risen Christ 'destroyed Rome'. Conceptual revolutions are as powerful as biological mutations. Douglas Murray and Ayaan Hirsi Ali moved away from reductive atheism; a Buddhist image shows the illuminated one above society and chaos.

## Key ideas (paraphrase)

- Despair about bringing children into the world is faithlessness, not kindness.
- Like Caleb and Joshua, keep faith in the future: choose life.
- Losing faith in being leads toward working against what thrives.
- The cure is not removing the serpents but raising people who can handle them.
- Conceptual revolutions transform humanity like mutations.
